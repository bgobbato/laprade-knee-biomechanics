Prone Dial Test Simulator (Bilateral Feet)

The Dial Test Rotational Stability

Evaluates side-to-side difference in tibial external rotation under a 5 N·m torque at both 30° and 90° of flexion in the prone position.

Key Diagnostic Criterion: An asymmetry of >10° at 30° flexion indicates an isolated PLC injury (Popliteus/PFL). Persistence or worsening at 90° indicates combined PCL + PLC rupture.
Varus Stress Test & Radiography Coronal Stability

Assesses lateral compartment gapping at 0° and 20°-30° flexion under a 10 N·m varus moment. Quantified on objective stress radiographs.

LaPrade Objective Thresholds:
• 2.7 mm delta: Isolated complete FCL tear.
• ≥ 4.0 mm delta: Complete Grade III PLC disruption (FCL + Popliteus + PFL).
Reverse Pivot Shift (Jakob / LaPrade) Posterolateral Subluxation

Begins at 80°-90° flexion with valgus and external rotation (causing posterolateral subluxation of the lateral tibial plateau) and extends towards 20°-30°.

Kinematic Mechanism: At 25°-30° flexion, the iliotibial band transitions from a flexor to an extensor, violently snapping the subluxated lateral plateau anteriorly into reduction.
Lachman & Pivot Shift (ACL + PLC) Anterior & Rotatory

Tests primary anterior translation (Lachman at 30°) and coupled rotatory subluxation (Pivot Shift). Demonstrates explosive Grade III shift when PLC is unaddressed.

The Graft Hazard: If PLC laxity is missed during ACL reconstruction, forces on the ACL graft surge by up to 230% under gait moments, ensuring premature failure.
External Rotation Recurvatum Test Hughston / LaPrade

Patient supine; great toes lifted. The knee with severe PLC or combined PCL/PLC tear falls into hyperextension, varus, and external rotation of the tibia.

Pathology: Loss of the posterior capsular and popliteofibular restraints permits unconstrained recurvatum and lateral dropoff.

The Anatomical & Biomechanical Legacy of Robert F. LaPrade & Jorge Chahla

Historically known as the "dark side of the knee" due to unpredictable outcomes of non-anatomic repairs and high rates of cruciate reconstruction failure, the posterolateral corner (PLC) was demystified through rigorous quantitative anatomical mapping and robotic sectioning studies by Dr. Robert F. LaPrade and his fellow Dr. Jorge Chahla (complex knee surgeon, originally from Tucumán, Argentina).

Their work proved that the Popliteus Tendon acts as the "fifth ligament" of the knee—a primary static and dynamic restraint against external tibial rotation—working in an inverted 'Y' construct with the Popliteofibular Ligament (PFL) and the Fibular Collateral Ligament (FCL). Crucially, they demonstrated that unaddressed posterolateral instability exposes ACL and PCL reconstruction grafts to catastrophic force spikes, leading to early graft elongation and failure.
